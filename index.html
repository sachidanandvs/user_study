What to look for

Each trial shows a text description and two videos (A and B) generated from it. They play in a loop. After both have played once, choose the video that is more physically realistic: the one whose events could more plausibly happen in the real world.

Signs that a video is physically unrealistic

✨
Objects appear out of nowhere
A ball, a hand, or a person pops into the scene with no cause.
💨
Objects disappear
Something vanishes, or a thrown object is gone mid-air.
〰️
Rigid things bend or melt
A bat, racket, table or bottle bends, stretches, wobbles or melts like rubber.
🧲
Motion without a force
An object starts moving, speeds up, changes direction or stops with nothing pushing it.
🎈
Gravity is wrong
Things float, hover, fall upward, or fall far too slowly or too fast.
👻
Objects pass through each other
A hand goes through a ball, a foot sinks into the floor, objects overlap and merge.
💥
Collisions look wrong
No reaction on impact, a bounce in the wrong direction, or a heavy object barely affected.
🔀
Objects change identity or number
One object morphs into another, or 2 balls become 3.
🦵
Impossible bodies
Extra or merging limbs, joints bending the wrong way, a body twisting impossibly.
💧
Liquids, cloth, smoke behave wrong
Water that doesn't flow or fill, liquid that acts solid, cloth that moves like a rigid sheet.
❓
Anything else that seems physically off
Trust your intuition about how the real world works.

Please keep in mind

  • Judge only physical realism. Ignore image sharpness, colours, style, resolution, smoothness of the frame rate, and black bars at the sides.
  • A video in which almost nothing moves is not automatically more realistic. If water is being poured but never moves, or a kicked ball never leaves the foot, that is unrealistic too.
  • Both videos may have problems. Pick the one with fewer or less severe physics errors.
  • Use "About the same" only when you really can't tell them apart in realism.
  • Use Replay as often as you like. Keyboard: A / S / B (or ← / ↓ / →), R = replay.
